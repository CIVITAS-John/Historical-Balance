# The Enemy of Our Enemy

When the Heavenly Kingdom rises, the Zheng court remembers the bargain with Wu Sangui: a rising on the mainland once let Tungning return to Fujian, but a common enemy gave no guarantee of a durable peace. This time Tungning can trade its arms industry and fleet for a promise of the coast.

## The last chance

- **Entry:** `fh_je_taiping_rebellion`. Detect the first actual `dp_taiping_rebellion` launched by the Heavenly Kingdom against Qing. Record the two governments and the specific conflict, with a catch-up check for an active matching play or war. The God Worshippers precursor, the date, and the bare existence of the Taiping tag are insufficient.
- **Unsettled claim:** Check first for a foothold already secured. Otherwise freeze [The Claim Across the Strait](01-mainland-claim.md#court-balance), cancel any provisional Qing offer, and force the court to choose **Take the Opportunity** or **Close the Book on the Mainland**. Stop normal actions, drift, and scheduled responses. Keep the decision in the journal if the popup is dismissed; it survives the rebellion ending unanswered.
- **Take the Opportunity:** Record reassertion, open the foothold entry, and send an envoy if the conflict is still active. Refusal, a blocked route, or the end of the rebellion leaves the court committed and free to pursue the foothold independently. The choice does not enter the war.
- **Close the Book on the Mainland:** Finalize renunciation without an offer to Qing. Permanently close the scripted mainland campaign and the Taiping aid route. The player can still use normal diplomacy and war goals.
- **Already settled:** A court that reasserted can open an optional Taiping audience without restarting mobilization or stopping its existing campaign. An existing foothold makes it an established partner, and it can bargain from strength. A court that already renounced stays out of this pact, even if it later takes mainland land. An earlier ratified Qing treaty stands.
- **AI:** At the fork, weigh the frozen bar, finances, legal aid routes, and the likelihood of Taiping accepting terms. Prefer abandonment when the court leans away from the mainland and cannot support a credible bid. Faith alone never decides the fork.

## Bargain with the Heavenly Kingdom

Use Tungning’s **current official religion** at the audience, rather than a pending nomination in [The Faith of the Court](02-court-faith.md). Give the AI Taiping court a starting willingness score; **4** accepts. Each position can reach 4 using the same concessions.

| Court religion | Willingness | Argument in the audience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protestant | 3 | Tungning can send a familiar delegation. Hong’s theology remains distinct from the island congregations. |
| Catholic | 2 | The delegates cite a shared Christian heritage. Clergy object to recognizing Hong’s religious claims. |
| Mahayana Buddhist | 1 | Negotiate through merchants and guarantee the protection of local worship in Tungning’s territory. |
| Confucian | 0 | Use a secular audience. Academies object to the pact and Taiping demands proof the court is serious. |

Add **+1** for relations of at least +20, **+1** for a credible offer of an arms shipment, and **+1** for a credible fleet commitment. **Recognize the Heavenly Kingdom as a wartime government** adds **+2** at the cost of −2 Devout and Landowner approval and −5 base legitimacy for one year when the pact is signed. This acknowledges a negotiating partner without recognizing its exclusive right to China. For an unlisted religion, use the secular audience starting at 1. A Taiping player can accept or refuse directly. Expire the offer after 90 days or when the conflict ends. Permit one revised offer after a refusal or expiry while the same conflict is still active.

### Bargaining from the mainland

Use the strongest position Tungning currently holds. Check ordinary land in Fujian, Guangdong, and Zhejiang; occupation alone, subject land, and treaty ports never count.

| Position | Willingness | What Tungning can ask for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Across the strait** | +0 | The standard bargain. A court that lost an earlier foothold sends former mainland magistrates as brokers, but must still earn the terms through aid. |
| **A landing on the coast** | +1 | Even without a whole region, directly held ordinary land is a base for talks. Taiping names the land Tungning already holds separately from the promise of future cessions. |
| **An established foothold** | +2 | One qualifying region under the [ownership rule](01-mainland-claim.md#mainland-foothold). Ask Taiping to recognize Tungning’s present coastal jurisdiction at signing and keep Taiping claims to those holdings out of any joint peace demands. Promise only the coastal holdings Tungning still lacks. |
| **A mainland government** | +3 | Two or more currently qualifying regions, or one with a registered and functioning [mainland administration](06-mainland-reconquest.md#consolidate-the-foothold). In addition, demand that trade privileges start at signing if legal, without waiting for the first delivery. If Tungning already holds all three regions, replace the cession schedule with recognition of the existing coast. |

A partial landing gives leverage even if the original claim was still open when Taiping rose. A full region acquired before the launch instead settles the claim first and unlocks the optional audience. If it arrives after the mandatory fork opens, it improves terms after **Take the Opportunity**. Recheck the position and agreed commitments on response. If the loss of a holding makes the terms unavailable, return the offer for revision without charging the concessions. Changes after ratification affect what can be delivered, but do not rewrite the treaty.

**Articles of the pact:** Taiping recognizes Tungning’s claim to Fujian, Guangdong, and Zhejiang and promises to cede its eligible holdings in the areas Tungning still lacks after a victory. It also promises **five years of Taiping → Tungning trade privileges**. Tungning commits to at least one material contribution: deliver arms, deploy naval assistance, or fight on an agreed front against Qing. If Tungning is already fighting the same Qing government in a separate war, Taiping can accept that front as its contribution without merging wars. Explain which commitments are feasible before presenting the offer. There is no requirement to convert, break a third-party treaty, or enter a new war; if subject restrictions or existing allegiances preclude every contribution, the audience can fail.

## Arms and ships

| Commitment | Requirement and cost | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Supply the arsenals** | Pay up to three shipments, six months apart. Require a reachable market and a functioning shipping route or adjacent market, enough exportable small arms, and a treasury out of default. Use one native goods-transfer article for six months of small arms when legal. If only the article is unavailable, allow funded procurement at a starting cost of 5% of Tungning’s gold reserve cap per shipment, still requiring the route and available arms. | Actual delivery counts toward the bargain and helps Taiping field its army. For funded procurement, use +10% army offense for six months instead of the native goods supply; these variants do not stack. A frozen or canceled article does not count as a shipment. |
| **Commit a squadron** | Use an available fleet with at least three combat ships, a reachable friendly port, and naval support permitted under existing diplomacy. The off-map commitment reduces Tungning’s naval power projection by 15% for six months and cannot be repeated while active. Alternatively, with the native ship transfer feature and a valid recipient port, donate an eligible combat ship permanently. | Mark naval assistance active for six months once escorts arrive or a ship transfers. Escorts reduce Taiping’s unit supply consumption by 10%. A blocked route or missing donor ships ends the escort and clears its modifiers. A donated ship remains Taiping’s. |
| **Back the Heavenly Kingdom** | If the original play still accepts backers and Tungning meets its involvement rules, offer to join the initiator with the normal risks of war. | Tungning fights with its own fleet. When counted as naval assistance, use a qualifying deployment in the theater in place of the off-map escort. Do not enter a war by script after the play closes. |

The player authorizes each contribution. The AI commits only if it can meet the delivery conditions without going into default and if the aid can improve Taiping’s position. Check current routes and war sides before each delivery. Do not give cash, ships, or buffs for a pledge alone.

## The Shanghai question

Offer **The Shanghai Roads** if Taiping has a reachable position on the lower Yangtze and the Qing side still controls ordinary Shanghai (`p:xB01101` in `STATE_SUZHOU`). Use actual city ownership and inspect treaty-port enclaves separately. If a foreign power owns the city outright, or Taiping already controls it, skip the assault opportunity.

- **A guarantee to the foreign settlements:** With both arms delivered and naval help active, Taiping can accept written guarantees for existing concessions and keep the squadron on the approach to Shanghai proper. For up to six months, give its army **+25% offense** for the Shanghai campaign, replacing the ordinary procurement bonus while this stronger modifier applies. Scope to the theater if supported; otherwise enable the country-wide modifier only during the opportunity and only if Taiping is fighting no other war, so it cannot help on another front. Aid still helps through the normal supply and fleet effects if Taiping refuses the guarantee.
- **Keep the fleet clear:** Tungning can limit itself to existing aid without risking a dispute with the treaty powers. No Shanghai bonus or automatic change to their relations or intervention.

End the theater effect when the opportunity, relevant aid, or original conflict ends. Even with full aid, Taiping must take the city through normal war and peace. Neither choice transfers treaty ports, compels neutrality, or opens a war against foreign powers. The fictional guarantee takes its cue from [Anson Burlingame’s 1862 dispatch on Shanghai and the treaty settlements](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1862/d671).

## Collect the promises

- **Trade:** After the first material contribution, or at signing under the terms for a mainland government, ratify or reuse the directed trade article as soon as both sides can legally do so. Show a blocked article as owed; allow ratification at settlement if previously blocked. Keep the duration to five years from activation for a newly signed article.
- **Qing defeat:** Stop wartime aid when the recorded conflict ends. If Taiping enforced victory over the Qing government in that conflict, or the conflict ended because that government dissolved and Taiping survives, prompt for the coastal settlement. A tag change alone is not dissolution. Honor each coastal promise only where Taiping itself owns ordinary land it can legally cede, Tungning earned the terms through a contribution, and the parties can ratify the transfer. Skip land already owned by Tungning. Transfer only eligible state scopes; if a whole-state transfer would include treaty-port or other ineligible land, list it as blocked. A Taiping player has 90 days to honor the terms; expiry counts as refusal. The AI normally honors a paid commitment if legal.
- **Armistice, defeat, or no deal:** If both governments survive a negotiated or white peace without Qing capitulating, no automatic coastal cession is due. Keep any valid trade article already in force. If Taiping falls or either side breaks the pact, cancel outstanding deliveries and decisions; a fallen country cannot fulfill a concession.
- **Aftermath:** Close the wartime entry when terms are resolved and record promises, fulfillment, and both governments’ rival claims. If Qing has fallen and both partners survive, show a final **Two Courts, One China** event foreshadowing their rivalry. This pass does not start a new conflict.
